Huh. The person that I have felt closest to my soul is not someone that instantly I became close with. So what happened? What *changed* that made us closer?

The answer, obviously, is many things: common interests, the occurence of unfortunate events that made us share pain and frustration, common topics we enjoy. But how did we *know* we had common interests? Why did I *know* this person went through an awful event? How did I *know* we both like to talk about what inspires us? The answer, in our case, was *architecture*. The space where we eat changed, and instead of being this huge space with huge tables and many people talking, it became a single table with a few chairs only. Usually, to get to know someone, you'd have to interact repeatedly. The possibilities of sitting together repeatedly with one person in the previous option were almost none, since the tables were huge; in the new one, it was a probability of 25% or so, yes, but also *it no longer mattered*. Because if we didn't sit together repeatedly with a single human, we were still close enough to hear what all of us seated were saying. It was *one* conversation. We kept one conversation everytime.

And I remember *that* one conversation in that table that probably inspired a text that night, which inspired another one, and so on. All cause we changed the space we eat in. The table became smaller: only a handful of seats, and those seats were pretty close even. The space also became quieter. We could *hear* each other. Architecture shapes our interactions. And I thank that fucking tiny table like nothing else.
