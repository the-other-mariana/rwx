Huh. Looking back at my life right now, I developed a very very unique relationship with another person. And a very deep one. Probably it's the person that I have felt closer to my soul ever in my life. But this has been going on for only a few months, and we have known each other for longer. So what happened? What *changed* that made us closer?

The answer, obviously, is many things: common interests, the occurence of unfortunate events that made us share pain and frustration, common topics we enjoy. But how did we *know* we had common interests? Why did I *know* this person went through an awful event? How did I *know* we both like to talk about what inspires us? The answer, in our case, was *architecture*. The space where we eat changed, and instead of being this huge space with huge tables and many people talking, it became a single table with a few chairs only. The possibilities of sitting together repeatedly with one person in the previous option were almost none, since the tables were huge; in the new one, it was a probability of 25% or so, yes, but also *it no longer mattered*. Because if we didn't sit together repeatedly with a single human, we were still close enough to hear what all of us seated were saying. It was *one* conversation. We kept one conversation everytime.

And I remember *that* one conversation that probably inspired a text that night, which continued everything else. All cause we changed the space we eat in. The table became smaller. The space became quieter. Architecture shapes our interactions. And I thank that fucking tiny table like nothing else.
